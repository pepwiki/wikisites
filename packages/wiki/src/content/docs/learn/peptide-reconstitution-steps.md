## How to Reconstitute Peptides

Peptide reconstitution is the process of dissolving lyophilized (freeze-dried) peptide powder into a liquid solution suitable for research use. Proper technique preserves peptide integrity, maintains sterility, and ensures accurate dosing. This guide provides a step-by-step protocol applicable to any lyophilized peptide.

## What Is Lyophilized Peptide?

Lyophilized peptides are supplied as white or off-white powders in sealed vials. Freeze-drying removes water while preserving the peptide's primary structure and biological activity. This format provides:

- **Extended shelf life:** 12–24 months at 2–8°C
- **Reduced degradation:** Minimal hydrolysis or oxidation
- **Transport stability:** No cold chain required during shipping
- **Accurate dosing:** Known mass in sealed vial

## Required Equipment

| Equipment | Purpose | Specification |
|-----------|---------|---------------|
| Bacteriostatic (BAC) water | Diluent | 0.9% benzyl alcohol, preservative-containing |
| Sterile syringe | Volume measurement | 1 mL or 3 mL |
| Needle | Drawing and injecting | 25–30 gauge, ½ inch |
| Alcohol swabs | Surface/vial disinfection | 70% isopropyl alcohol |
| Clean workspace | Contamination prevention | Laminar flow hood or clean bench preferred |

### Why BAC Water?

Bacteriostatic water contains 0.9% benzyl alcohol, which inhibits bacterial growth. This is critical because:

- Reconstituted peptides lack preservatives beyond the BAC water
- Multiple doses may be drawn from the same vial over days to weeks
- Sterile water (without preservative) provides no antimicrobial protection
- Saline or other diluents may alter peptide stability or pH

## Step-by-Step Reconstitution Protocol

### Step 1: Prepare Workspace

1. Clean and sanitize a flat work surface
2. Gather all equipment within reach
3. Wash hands thoroughly
4. Ensure adequate lighting

### Step 2: Inspect the Vial

1. Examine the lyophilized powder for color (should be white to off-white)
2. Check for clumping or discoloration (indicates moisture exposure)
3. Verify the vial seal is intact
4. Confirm the expiration date

### Step 3: Clean the Vial Stopper

1. Remove the plastic flip-off cap from the vial
2. Swab the rubber stopper with 70% isopropyl alcohol
3. Allow to air dry completely (15–30 seconds)
4. Do not touch the stopper after cleaning

### Step 4: Prepare BAC Water

1. Swab the BAC water vial stopper with 70% isopropyl alcohol
2. Allow to air dry
3. Draw the desired volume of BAC water into the syringe
4. Hold syringe needle-up and expel air bubbles

### Step 5: Inject BAC Water into Peptide Vial

1. Insert the needle through the center of the rubber stopper
2. **Inject slowly** — direct the stream down the vial wall, not onto the powder
3. Rapid injection causes foaming and can denature the peptide
4. Remove the needle and syringe after injection

### Step 6: Dissolve the Peptide

1. **Do not shake the vial** — shaking introduces air bubbles and can denature the peptide
2. Gently swirl the vial in a circular motion
3. Tilt the vial side to side to wet all powder
4. Continue until the solution is **clear and colorless** (or characteristic color)
5. Typical dissolution time: 1–5 minutes

### Step 7: Verify Complete Dissolution

1. Hold the vial against a light source
2. Confirm no visible particles or undissolved powder
3. Solution should be clear (may be slightly colored for some peptides)
4. If particles remain, gently swirl again; do not shake

### Step 8: Draw the Dose

1. Swab the vial stopper with alcohol
2. Invert the vial and insert the needle
3. Draw the calculated dose volume
4. Expel air bubbles by tapping the syringe
5. Verify the correct volume against the dose calculation

## Common Reconstitution Scenarios

### Standard Reconstitution Table

| Vial Mass | BAC Water Volume | Final Concentration | Common Use |
|-----------|------------------|---------------------|------------|
| 5 mg | 5 mL | 1 mg/mL | General research |
| 5 mg | 2 mL | 2.5 mg/mL | High-concentration |
| 5 mg | 10 mL | 0.5 mg/mL | Low-concentration |
| 2 mg | 2 mL | 1 mg/mL | Standard |
| 10 mg | 10 mL | 1 mg/mL | Larger vials |

### Dose Calculation Formula

```
Volume (mL) = Desired Dose (mg) ÷ Concentration (mg/mL)
```

**Example:** 500 µg dose from a 1 mg/mL solution:
```
Volume = 0.5 mg ÷ 1 mg/mL = 0.5 mL (50 units on a U-100 insulin syringe)
```

## Common Mistakes and Prevention

| Mistake | Consequence | Prevention |
|---------|-------------|------------|
| Shaking the vial | Peptide denaturation, foaming | Swirl gently only |
| Injecting BAC water too fast | Foaming, aggregation | Inject slowly down vial wall |
| Using non-sterile water | Microbial contamination | Always use BAC water |
| Touching the stopper | Contamination | Use alcohol swabs, no-touch technique |
| Using expired BAC water | Reduced preservative efficacy | Check expiration date |
| Incorrect dilution | Dosing errors | Calculate concentration before reconstitution |
| Freezing reconstituted peptide | Degradation, aggregation | Store at 2–8°C only |

## Post-Reconstitution Storage

| Parameter | Recommendation |
|-----------|----------------|
| Storage temperature | 2–8°C (refrigerator) |
| Maximum storage | 30 days (with BAC water) |
| Light exposure | Protect from light |
| Freeze-thaw | Do not freeze |
| Container | Original vial with stopper |

## Aseptic Technique Tips

1. Work in a clean, draft-free environment
2. Use a new syringe and needle for each reconstitution
3. Do not re-use BAC water vials across multiple peptide vials
4. If multi-dosing, swab the stopper before each draw
5. Label the vial with peptide name, concentration, and reconstitution date
