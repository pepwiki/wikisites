## Insulin Lispro vs Aspart — Rapid-Acting Comparison

Insulin lispro (Humalog) and insulin aspart (NovoLog/Novorapid) are two rapid-acting insulin analogues used to manage postprandial glucose excursions. Both provide faster onset and shorter duration than regular human insulin, but differ in their pharmacokinetic profiles, formulation options, and clinical applications.

## Molecular Differences

| Property | Insulin Lispro | Insulin Aspart |
|----------|---------------|----------------|
| Brand names | Humalog, Admelog | NovoLog, Novorapid |
| Manufacturer | Eli Lilly | Novo Nordisk |
| Sequence modification | ProB28→Lys, LysB29→Pro | ProB28→Asp |
| Molecular weight | 5,808 Da | 5,826 Da |
| Structural effect | Disrupted dimer formation | Disrupted dimer formation |
| Concentration | U-100, U-200 | U-100, U-200 |

Both analogues disrupt the hexameric self-association of insulin through B28/B29 modifications, resulting in faster absorption from subcutaneous tissue.

## Pharmacokinetic Comparison

| Parameter | Insulin Lispro (U-100) | Insulin Aspart (U-100) |
|-----------|----------------------|----------------------| 
| Onset | 5–15 minutes | 5–15 minutes |
| Peak | 30–90 minutes | 30–90 minutes |
| Duration | 3–5 hours | 3–5 hours |
| Time to 50% AUC | ~1.5 hours | ~1.5 hours |
| Bioavailability | 55–75% | 55–75% |

### PK/PD Differences

| Metric | Lispro | Aspart | Clinical Significance |
|--------|--------|--------|----------------------|
| Maximum plasma concentration (Tmax) | ~60 min | ~60 min | Similar postprandial coverage |
| Early glucose control (0–60 min) | Slightly faster | Slightly slower | Marginal difference |
| Duration of action | 3–5 hours | 3–5 hours | Similar basal coverage |

In clinical practice, the pharmacokinetic differences between lispro and aspart are generally considered negligible for most patients.

## Clinical Comparison

### Dosing Equivalence

- **1 unit of insulin lispro ≈ 1 unit of insulin aspart**
- No dose conversion required when switching between analogues
- Both are dosed based on carbohydrate ratio and correction factor

### Postprandial Glucose Control

| Scenario | Lispro | Aspart |
|----------|--------|--------|
| Pre-meal dosing (0–15 min before) | Standard | Standard |
| Post-meal dosing (0–20 min after) | Standard | Standard |
| Insulin pump use | Standard | Standard |
| Closed-loop systems | Compatible | Compatible |

### Hypoglycemia Risk

| Time Period | Lispro | Aspart |
|-------------|--------|--------|
| Early postprandial (0–2 hours) | Low | Low |
| Late postprandial (2–5 hours) | Moderate | Moderate |
| Nocturnal (when dosed at dinner) | Low | Low |

Both analogues have similar hypoglycemia profiles when dosed appropriately.

## Switching Between Analogues

### Conversion Guidelines

- **Direct 1:1 conversion** is appropriate for most patients
- Monitor glucose patterns for 3–5 days after switching
- Adjust basal insulin if needed
- Recalculate insulin-to-carb ratios if switching ultra-rapid formulations

### Considerations for Switching

| Factor | Recommendation |
|--------|----------------|
| Pump users | Consider pump cartridge compatibility |
| Cost | Compare biosimilar availability |
| Ultra-rapid needs | Lyumjev or Fiasp may be preferred |
| Mix formulations | Different mix ratios may require dose adjustment |
