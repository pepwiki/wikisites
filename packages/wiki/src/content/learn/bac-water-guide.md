## BAC Water for Peptides

Bacteriostatic water (BAC water) is the standard diluent for reconstituting lyophilized peptides. This guide covers composition, selection criteria, storage requirements, and best practices.

## Composition

| Component | Concentration | Function |
|-----------|---------------|----------|
| Water for injection | q.s. to 100% | Solvent |
| Benzyl alcohol | 0.9% (9 mg/mL) | Antimicrobial preservative |

**pH:** 4.5–7.0 (typically ~5.0–6.0)

**Osmolarity:** Approximately isotonic with body fluids

## Why BAC Water

### Benzyl Alcohol Preservative

The 0.9% benzyl alcohol concentration provides:

- **Bacteriostatic activity:** Inhibits bacterial growth after multi-dose vial access
- **Extended shelf life:** Opened vials remain usable for 28 days
- **Safety:** Well-tolerated at standard injection volumes

### Advantages Over Alternatives

| Diluent | Preservative | Multi-Dose Safe | Shelf Life After Opening |
|---------|-------------|----------------|-------------------------|
| Bacteriostatic water | Benzyl alcohol 0.9% | Yes | 28 days |
| Normal saline | None | No | 24 hours |
| Sterile water | None | No | Use immediately |

## BAC Water vs Alternatives

### Normal Saline (0.9% NaCl)

- **Composition:** 0.9% sodium chloride in water
- **Advantage:** Isotonic,生理相容性 better for sensitive tissues
- **Disadvantage:** No preservative — single-use only
- **Use case:** Peptides sensitive to benzyl alcohol

### Sterile Water for Injection

- **Composition:** Water only, no additives
- **Advantage:** Preservative-free
- **Disadvantage:** No antimicrobial protection
- **Use case:** Immediate use, preservative sensitivity

### Dilute Acetic Acid

- **Composition:** 0.2% acetic acid
- **Advantage:** Maintains peptide solubility for certain formulations
- **Disadvantage:** pH may affect stability
- **Use case:** Insulin and specific peptide formulations

## Selection Criteria

### Choose BAC Water When:

- Multi-dose vial access is planned
- Reconstituted peptide will be stored >24 hours
- Standard peptide reconstitution protocol
- No preservative sensitivity

### Choose Normal Saline When:

- Peptide is sensitive to benzyl alcohol
- Single-use reconstitution
- Intravenous compatibility required
- Preservative-free formulation needed

### Choose Sterile Water When:

- Immediate use only
- Preservative sensitivity confirmed
- Pediatric or neonatal applications (benzyl alcohol contraindicated)

## Storage Requirements

### Unopened BAC Water

| Parameter | Requirement |
|-----------|------------|
| Temperature | 20–25°C (room temperature) |
| Light | Protect from direct light |
| Freezing | Do not freeze |
| Shelf life | 24–36 months (manufacturer dependent) |

### Opened BAC Water

| Parameter | Requirement |
|-----------|------------|
| Storage | 2–8°C after opening |
| Maximum use | 28 days from first puncture |
| Documentation | Mark date opened on vial |
| Discard | After 28 days or if contaminated |

## Reconstitution Protocol

### Step-by-Step

1. **Allow BAC water to reach room temperature** (5–10 minutes)
2. **Clean vial stopper** with 70% isopropyl alcohol swab
3. **Draw appropriate volume** into syringe
4. **Inject slowly down the vial wall** — never directly onto lyophilized cake
5. **Gently swirl** until dissolved (do not shake)
6. **Inspect solution** — must be clear and colorless
7. **Label reconstituted vial** with peptide name, concentration, date, and expiration

### Volume Selection

| Desired Concentration | BAC Water per 5 mg Vial |
|----------------------|------------------------|
| 1 mg/mL | 5 mL |
| 2 mg/mL | 2.5 mL |
| 0.5 mg/mL | 10 mL |

## Quality Considerations

### What to Look For

| Parameter | Good Quality | Reject If |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| Clarity | Clear, colorless | Cloudy, tinted |
| Particles | None visible | Any floating material |
| Seal integrity | Intact | Loose or damaged |
| Expiration | Within date | Expired |
| Storage history | Proper temp | Heat exposure suspected |

### Contamination Indicators

- Visible particles or turbidity
- Discoloration
- Floating material
- Strong or unusual odor
- Open or damaged packaging

## Safety Considerations

### Benzyl Alcohol Sensitivity

- Rare but possible hypersensitivity reactions
- Symptoms: Localized swelling, redness, itching
- Management: Discontinue use, switch to preservative-free diluent

### Neonatal Considerations

- Benzyl alcohol contraindicated in neonates ("gasping syndrome")
- Use sterile water or preservative-free saline for pediatric populations

### Injection Best Practices

- Use aseptic technique for all reconstitutions
- Clean workspace and supplies
- Alcohol swab all vial stoppers
- Do not touch needle or syringe tip
- Dispose of sharps properly

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I use expired BAC water?

No. Expired bacteriostatic water may have reduced preservative efficacy and should not be used for peptide reconstitution.

### Can I freeze BAC water?

No. Freezing may compromise the preservative and physical properties. Store at room temperature (unopened) or 2–8°C (opened).

### How long does reconstituted peptide last?

Most peptides reconstituted with BAC water are stable for 30 days at 2–8°C. Sermorelin is an exception — use within 48 hours.

### Can I mix BAC water and normal saline?

Not recommended unless specifically directed by a protocol. Mixing may alter pH and osmolarity.
