## Overview

Peptide drug costs vary widely based on molecule complexity, manufacturing process, indication, and market dynamics. This reference compiles per-dose, monthly, and annual costs for major therapeutic peptides, including projected biosimilar savings and international pricing comparisons.

## Cost Metrics

- **WAC (Wholesale Acquisition Cost)**: Manufacturer list price before discounts
- **Net price**: After rebates, discounts, and PBM negotiations
- **Per-dose cost**: Cost per single administration
- **Monthly cost**: WAC × dosing frequency per month
- **Annual cost**: Monthly cost × 12
- **Out-of-pocket**: Patient cost after insurance (variable)

## Cost Drivers

### Manufacturing

- **Peptide synthesis**: $100–500/gram (solid-phase)
- **Recombinant production**: $50–200/gram (for biologics)
- **Purification**: 30–50% of total manufacturing cost
- **Quality control**: 10–20% of total manufacturing cost
- **Formulation**: 15–25% of total manufacturing cost

### Market Factors

- **Patent protection**: 12–20 years from first filing
- **Market exclusivity**: 7–12 years (biologics)
- **Pricing power**: High for life-sustaining therapies
- **Payer negotiation**: Rebates 20–50% off WAC
- **Government regulation**: Price caps in EU, Japan, Australia

## Summary

Peptide drug costs remain high, driven by manufacturing complexity, patent protection, and market dynamics. GLP-1 agonists for obesity ($12,000–16,000/year) represent the largest cost category, with biosimilar entry expected to reduce prices 20–30% by 2030. Insulin prices have stabilized with biosimilar competition and US price caps. International pricing shows 3–5× lower costs in non-US markets.
