## Overview

Peptide stability is a critical parameter for research and clinical applications. This reference compiles degradation rates, storage conditions, and shelf life data for over 100 peptides, organized by peptide class and application. Data sources include published literature, manufacturer specifications, and regulatory submissions.

## Stability Metrics

- **t90**: Time to 90% purity (remaining intact peptide)
- **Degradation rate**: % loss per month under specified conditions
- **Primary degradation pathways**: Deamidation, oxidation, hydrolysis, aggregation, racemization
- **Storage temperature**: Optimal temperature for long-term storage
- **Reconstitution stability**: Time peptide remains viable after reconstitution

## Degradation Pathways

### Deamidation

- **Mechanism**: Asn/Gln → Asp/Glu via succinimide intermediate
- **Conditions**: Accelerated by pH 5–8, elevated temperature
- **Detection**: Mass spectrometry (+1 Da), RP-HPLC
- **Mitigation**: Low pH storage, N-terminal acetylation

### Oxidation

- **Mechanism**: Met, Cys, Trp, Tyr oxidation by ROS
- **Conditions**: Light, oxygen, metal ions, elevated temperature
- **Detection**: Mass spectrometry (+16 Da), RP-HPLC
- **Mitigation**: Inert atmosphere, antioxidants, dark storage

### Aggregation

- **Mechanism**: β-sheet formation, fibrillation, amorphous aggregates
- **Conditions**: Concentration, temperature, agitation, surfaces
- **Detection**: DLS, SEC, turbidity, SDS-PAGE
- **Mitigation**: Low concentration, surfactants, lyophilization

### Hydrolysis

- **Mechanism**: Peptide bond cleavage at acid-labile sites (Asp-Pro)
- **Conditions**: Low pH, elevated temperature
- **Detection**: RP-HPLC, mass spectrometry
- **Mitigation**: Avoid acidic pH at Asp-Pro sites

## Best Practices

### Storage

1. Aliquot upon receipt to avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles
2. Use low-protein-binding microcentrifuge tubes
3. Store lyophilized peptides under desiccant at −20°C or −80°C
4. Avoid light exposure (use amber tubes or wrap in foil)
5. Record freeze-thaw cycle count

### Reconstitution

1. Allow lyophilized peptide to equilibrate to room temperature before opening
2. Reconstitute slowly along the tube wall to avoid foaming
3. Use recommended solvent (typically water or dilute acetic acid)
4. Gently invert or vortex briefly — do not shake vigorously
5. Prepare single-use aliquots immediately after reconstitution

### Quality Control

1. Verify peptide identity by mass spectrometry upon receipt
2. Check purity by RP-HPLC (>95% for research, >98% for clinical)
3. Store certificates of analysis with peptide inventory
4. Monitor peptide stability at regular intervals
5. Discard peptides showing turbidity, discoloration, or precipitation
